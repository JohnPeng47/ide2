<task>
Model

- HierGraph = (V, T, E)
  - V is a finite set of nodes.
  - T is a set of ordered pairs from V x V. The graph (V, T) is directed and acyclic.
  - E is also a set of ordered pairs from V x V. Its relations may form cycles.
  - Vertical (compositional). (whole, part) in T means part is a direct part of whole. Following T downward moves from a whole into its parts. A shared part can have several parents and retain one identity.
  - Horizontal (relational). (u, v) in E records a relation from u to v. Its meaning comes from the particular graph. The endpoints can occur at different vertical levels.
  - A root has no incoming T edge. A leaf has no outgoing T edge. Multiple roots, shared descendants, disconnected components, and isolated nodes are all valid.

Task

You are given a diagram that compresses the core of a codebase into a single dense view: the connected set of responsibilities around which the application's main behavior and state are organized. Many components share one tree. Their composition is flattened and their relations are mentioned only in passing.

Your task is to propose a plan for spreading this coverage out along two dimensions:
- Vertically, by decomposing nodes into their parts, i.e. extending T downward.
- Horizontally, by tracing the relations between nodes, i.e. extending E.

The plan should say where the diagram is too compressed in each dimension and how to open it up.

Shape of the plan

The plan opens the diagram up with a mix of three moves. Use each one where it fits. You do not need to use all three.

1. New diagrams on the same canvas
   Add a diagram next to the input diagram when it elaborates one of the input's nodes or relations in place. Anchor it to the exact node it elaborates.
   - Vertical: the diagram decomposes the anchor node into its parts.
   - Horizontal: the diagram traces a relation, or a chain of relations, that starts at or passes through the anchor.

2. Child canvases
   Open a new canvas below a node when that node's own composition and internal relations are rich enough to need a canvas of their own. A child canvas is one T step down from its anchor node: its diagrams together describe that node.

3. Same-level canvases
   Open a new canvas beside the current one when a body of related responsibilities sits at the same level as the core and is linked to it mainly through E, e.g. a subsystem the core calls, feeds or listens to. State which relations connect it to the current canvas.

For each canvas in the plan, the current one included, describe:
- Each diagram: what it covers, which dimension it extends, and which node or relation it is anchored to.
- How the diagrams relate: the composition (T) and relation (E) links between them, each with a direction and a short meaning, plus the links to diagrams on other canvases.

Ground every proposal in the source. Point to the input diagram's nodes where it is compressed, and to the files that show the composition or relation being uncovered. Do not propose diagrams that repeat each other's coverage.
